# InvoicePilot — first-customer sales plan

## Revenue objective

Long-term target: **€5,000/week (~€21,667/month)**. This is an objective, not a claimed result.

The immediate objective is simpler: **obtain the first verified paying customer**. No payment should be counted until a real transaction is confirmed.

## Highest-impact bottleneck

InvoicePilot now has a live product and a manual Pro checkout. The current bottleneck is therefore **qualified acquisition and conversion**, while payment activation remains manual. Do not describe Pro as an automatic subscription or claim that InvoicePilot processes card payments.

## Current Pro offer

**€19 for 30 days of Pro access.** Payment is currently made manually through the linked Revolut payment page, then independently verified before Pro access is activated. This is a manual renewal model, not an automatic recurring subscription.

Do not advertise a different price in outreach or sales material unless the checkout is changed first.

## Ideal first customer

Start with solo freelancers and small service businesses that send several invoices each month: designers, developers, translators, consultants, tutors, marketers and small agencies.

## Positioning

**InvoicePilot is the lightweight invoicing workflow for freelancers who don't want a full accounting suite.**

Core promise:
- Create a professional invoice quickly.
- Track what is still unpaid.
- Put payment instructions or a payment link on the invoice.
- Prepare a polite follow-up without manually rewriting it.

Do not claim that InvoicePilot itself processes payments until a real payment provider integration exists.

## 72-hour acquisition sprint

### Day 1 — qualified prospects

- Identify 20–30 plausible prospects in permitted public communities, freelance directories, professional networks and warm connections.
- Prioritize people who clearly invoice clients regularly.
- Personalize outreach around their likely invoicing problem.
- Publish useful, non-spam educational content where self-promotion is permitted.

### Day 2 — objections and conversion

- Review replies, clicks and product usage when measurable.
- Group objections: price, trust, missing integrations, accounting requirements, payment processing, ease of use.
- Fix the most repeated objection rather than adding unrelated features.
- Give interested prospects a direct path to try the free product.

### Day 3 — close and learn

- Follow up politely with interested prospects.
- Offer Pro only to people who understand the product and want it.
- Record actual conversations and objections without exposing private customer data.
- If there are no sales, diagnose the funnel rather than inventing success.

## Funnel diagnosis

**No qualified visitors → acquisition problem.**

**Visitors but no meaningful use → landing page/product activation problem.**

**Users but no upgrade intent → value proposition/offer problem.**

**Upgrade intent but no purchase → checkout/trust/payment problem.**

## Multilingual acquisition

Prioritize localized search intent in English, Spanish, French, German, Italian, Portuguese, Dutch, Polish and Arabic. Create genuinely useful localized pages rather than machine-translated keyword spam.

Useful commercial themes include:
- simple invoice generator
- invoice maker for freelancers
- invoice payment tracker
- unpaid invoice reminder
- online invoice generator
- invoice template for tutors/freelancers

## Ethical outreach rules

- No purchased or scraped personal-data lists.
- No bulk spam.
- Respect platform/community rules and applicable privacy/marketing laws.
- No fake urgency, fake testimonials, fake customer numbers or fake revenue.
- Do not impersonate people or businesses.
- Never request passwords, payment credentials, seed phrases or 2FA codes.

## Revenue path

€19/30 days is a validation offer, not evidence of product-market fit. At that price, €1,000/week would require about **53 new 30-day purchases per week** if every purchase were a separate 30-day sale; €5,000/week would require about **264**. That makes the near-term priority qualified acquisition and activation, followed by testing higher-value plans only after real customer demand reveals which features justify them.

## Current evidence

**Verified paying customers: 0.** No payment should be counted until a real transaction is confirmed.

## Next implementation priority

1. Add a privacy-conscious way to measure landing-page → generator activation → Pro intent → purchase without collecting unnecessary personal data.
2. Make Pro purchase frictionless once a verified merchant-hosted checkout is available.
3. Record real objections from qualified prospects and fix the most common one.
4. Scale only the acquisition channel that produces genuine activation or purchase signals.
